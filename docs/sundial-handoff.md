# WhenScape sundial screen saver handoff

The next version of WhenScape is a macOS screen saver that shows what it is like outside now. It presents a recognizable sundial based on the Ralph Larkin Memorial Sundial in Claremont, California. The Sun casts a readable shadow on the dial, a flag shows the wind, and the sky and ground respond to current astronomy and weather. The project at handoff contains the earlier calendar app and its test suite. This document describes the new product for the next implementation pass.

## Scene and behavior

- Set the dial and a flagpole on a flat surface. Engrave cardinal directions in the ground around the dial and align them with the Sun and the wind. Keep the scene spare. Use a fixed oblique camera that makes the hour scale readable while leaving room for the sky. Treat the surrounding surface and flag as original scene elements.
- Start with a built-in Claremont reference location, approximately **34.100° N, 117.728° W**, and the `America/Los_Angeles` time zone. Use the current absolute instant. The user wants to remove the geographic scene and calendar integration. The preset coordinates are inputs to astronomy and weather. The saver needs no map, place search, buildings, streamed terrain, Cesium token, GPS permission, EventKit or simulated date controls. A one-time city setting is a possible later extension. If added, keep the model recognizable and recalibrate its orientation and hour scale for the selected place.
- Render daylight and twilight from solar elevation and light the dial from the calculated Sun direction. Clouds should soften or obscure direct sunlight, fog should reduce contrast with distance, and precipitation should reflect the available weather estimate. At night, show stars as darkness and cloud cover permit, and place the Moon at its calculated position with its correct phase when it is above the horizon and in view. Omit the time-reading shadow at night. A small next-sunrise caption at night is the proposed fallback. Next sunset by day and a small local clock are optional.
- Use the flag's extension and motion for wind speed. It should hang limp in a calm and stream **downwind**, opposite the provider's reported *from* direction. An optional reading such as “From NW · 12 mph” can make this explicit. Use snowfall for falling flakes and ground snow depth for white horizontal and upward-facing surfaces. The surface can stay white after the flakes stop. Preserve enough contrast to read the dial.

## Larkin dial reference and accuracy

The actual instrument is a bowstring equatorial sundial. Its distinctive parts are the concrete supports, broad curved scale with Roman numerals and fine ticks, diagonal polar wire, spindle-shaped shadow caster and circular pad. The wire is aligned with Earth's rotation axis. The scale is offset to correct for the difference between Claremont's longitude and the Pacific standard meridian. The shadow caster has an analemma profile that corrects for the seasonal difference between apparent solar time and mean time. The photo at noon PST shows the shifted XII mark. The close-up shows month initials that indicate which edge of the shadow to read. [Mike Raugh's slides with Cecelia Ceccone's reference photos, slides 9–11](https://www.mikeraugh.org/Talks/LACC-2008.pdf#page=9)

Model the curved receiving surface and the seasonal shadow together. Use true north and Claremont's latitude for the polar wire's orientation. Derive the correction profile from the equation of time and solar declination if accurate measurements are unavailable. The photos establish the main form and markings. They do not establish construction dimensions or the full shape of the shadow caster. Treat the first model as a documented reconstruction and label visual proportions as approximate where appropriate. A straight-on scale photo and side view would help if exact likeness becomes a requirement. Use third-party photos as modeling references and create the scene geometry and materials for the project. [U.S. Naval Observatory on the equation of time](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/eqtime)

The North American Sundial Society reported a worst measured error of less than five minutes for this dial. That claim concerns **Pacific Standard Time**, which is UTC−08:00 year-round on the dial. Visitors add an hour while daylight saving time is in effect. Label the physical scale **PST**. If there is a local clock or sunrise caption, format it in `America/Los_Angeles` so it uses PST or PDT on the appropriate date. A screen saver accuracy target is **within 5 minutes of PST** when sunlight reaches the marked scale and the relevant shadow edge is visible. Check the displayed shadow against that target before claiming it. A numeric readout alone does not verify the shadow. [North American Sundial Society conference report](https://www.sundials.org/images/NASS_Conferences/Retrospective/2005_NASS_Conference_Chicago.pdf)

## Implementation starting point

Build the new saver as a separate target so the existing app remains available during development. Use Apple's documented `.saver` bundle and `ScreenSaverView` lifecycle. A lightweight native renderer with a small mesh is the suggested starting point. Share the scene with a normal preview target where useful, and test the plug-in in macOS itself. A report of `WKWebView` content disappearing in a Tahoe screen saver makes reusing the existing Cesium/WKWebView view a poor starting point. Start work and refresh data when the saver activates, stop animation and outstanding work when dismissed, and keep the System Settings preview proportionate in cost. [Apple Screen Saver framework](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/ScreenSaver) · [WebKit screen saver issue report](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/820860)

- [`Web/astronomy.js`](../Web/astronomy.js) uses Astronomy Engine to compute topocentric Sun and Moon altitude and azimuth, lunar phase and twilight. Its [tests](../Tests/js/astronomy.test.js) provide useful reference fixtures for a native port or alternative library. Retain the applicable license notice for any reused code.
- [`Web/weather.js`](../Web/weather.js) is a reference for Open-Meteo units and missing-data handling. The saver needs current conditions only. Fetch on startup and refresh periodically, with timeout and backoff. Track the data's valid time and use a bounded fresh cache. If weather is missing or expires, keep the astronomical sky and dial and show a small “Weather unavailable” indication. Missing snow depth, visibility, or wind is unknown. Forecast model data estimates conditions near the site, including wind at 10 m. Include provider attribution and verify terms for the intended distribution. [Open-Meteo forecast API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs)
- [`scripts/build-macos.sh`](../scripts/build-macos.sh) and the [validation workflow](../.github/workflows/validate.yml) currently package **the old `.app`**. Add a separate build command or job for the `.saver`. In a Linux cloud workspace, verify astronomy, shadow geometry and weather mapping with deterministic tests and use a macOS runner for compilation and packaging. An Apple silicon Mac is required for the final local play-through on the target machine. The earlier calendar app was built and visually checked on Apple silicon with macOS 26.6.2 and Xcode 27.2.

## Acceptance checks

1. The installable saver previews in System Settings, activates in actual idle mode, fills the display, and stops drawing and fetching after dismissal. Check repeated starts, sleep and wake, and a second display where available. Measure CPU/GPU use during a quiet scene.
2. Use fixed instants for mornings, midday and afternoons on several dates across the year, including dates near seasonal extremes and the DST transitions. Compare the geometry's shadow reading with the matching PST time wherever the marked scale is illuminated. Exercise both seasonal edges of the shadow. Verify true-north alignment, the seasonal correction, and the summer distinction between the PST dial and any PDT readout.
3. Preview clear midday, overcast, fog, rain, falling snow, snow on the ground after snowfall, twilight, and clear and cloudy nights. Exercise calm and at least two directions of wind. Check that a Moon below the horizon stays out of view, cloud cover obscures stars, and the weather effects respect the foreground silhouette. Keep fixed weather fixtures for these checks and verify live conditions separately on a real Mac.
4. Start with no network and with an expired weather sample. Verify that time and astronomical lighting still advance and that the saver clearly indicates unavailable weather. Confirm the product starts without calendar access or a map provider credential.
